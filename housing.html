Home Value Estimator

Can structural and neighborhood features alone give a real-estate desk a quick, explainable value estimate — without relying on a black box?

The situation

506 homes, 14 features. The goal isn't just low error — it's knowing which inputs actually carry the signal, so a lightweight valuation tool collects the right data instead of everything available.

Note: this is the historical Boston Housing benchmark (1978), not current market data, and the race-derived B feature was excluded from modeling on ethical grounds.

Key takeaway & action

1

Two features carry ~83% of the predictive weight — and the "obvious" ones don't

RM (average rooms per dwelling) and LSTAT (% lower-status population in the neighborhood) together account for roughly 83% of feature importance. CRIM (per-capita crime rate) — the feature most people would guess matters most — contributes under 4%, similar to tax rate and distance to employment centers.

ActionFor a lightweight valuation tool, prioritize accurate room-count and neighborhood socioeconomic data collection; crime statistics and tax records add comparatively little predictive value and can be deprioritized if collection cost is a constraint.

Limitations

  • Boston Housing is a 1978 benchmark dataset — not appropriate for current property pricing or policy decisions.
  • The race-derived B feature was excluded from modeling and from this analysis entirely.
  • 506 rows is a small sample for a 12-feature model; importance rankings can shift with more data.
